§ 1

What changed

The square patch is gone, and it was mine. Board A kept foil off a box at the top-left so the rank and suit stayed legible — a knockout you could see as a cut-out around the 4♠. The fix is not a softer mask but a different blend: on white paper multiply cannot lighten anything, so the card takes the foil colour exactly while near-black ink comes through untouched. The plate now runs edge to edge under the glyphs with nothing to cut around. Night stock is the opposite problem and screens instead.

Fan curvature is deleted, not zeroed. It existed to make a highlight walk the hand, and it didn't pay for itself. Every card now shares one normal, which has a consequence worth stating: tilt can no longer light one card more than its neighbour, so tilt-as-brightness had nowhere to go.

So the twist moves the pattern instead. Tilting rotates the ruled lines and slides them across the cards; brightness holds still. The light is sized and floored so nothing on the page can go dark at any angle — the blade keeps a visible bright edge, but its dark side is now a lit sheen rather than bare paper.

The rig

Fewer dials than board A, because most of them were answering questions you have since closed. What is left is the palette, the two line dials, and the two that decide how evenly the card is lit.

§ 4

The tilt

Set INPUT to DEVICE. The first reading is the rest pose — hold the phone however is comfortable and that becomes centre. From there, twisting rotates the rules by up to about twenty degrees and slides them roughly a card's width. Nothing dims. Every card on this page is lit at every angle; what the phone changes is which way the grain runs.

Smoothing is a 190 ms time constant, derived per frame from real elapsed time so it filters the same at 30, 60 and 120 Hz. The shine is a derivative of the angle, so tremor invisible in the phone's position is very visible in the light without it.

On a desktop, POINTER feeds the same path from the mouse and dragging the felt does the same with a thumb. ?device=1 stands the page up in the phone's rest pose.

§ 5

The gallery

Tight sweeps around where you landed — weight 0.72, count 0.40 — rather than board A's full grid. Every tile answers the phone, and all of them share one tilt, so the axis being compared stays the only difference between two of them.

§ 6

Notes

The palettes

All four run in both stocks. SPECTRUM is board A's treatment, kept as the reference — it is what night looks like today and what light mode looked like when you called it rainbowy. DUOTONE clamps the hue to a cyan–magenta arc, which is the range holographic film actually occupies and where most of the rainbow feeling was coming from. LOW SAT keeps the full wheel at about a quarter of the saturation. METALS abandons hue rotation for a verdigris-to-gold ramp — the app's own accent pair, and the only one that reads as printing rather than as film. DUOTONE is the default; if it holds up at night too, that is one palette instead of two.

The glyphs

Worth checking specifically, since it is what the knockout was protecting. Under multiply the rank and suit are mathematically untouched on day stock — you cannot lighten by multiplying. Night is not free in the same way: screen lifts the light glyphs along with the card, so contrast compresses a little at high INTENSITY. If a night card ever reads soft, that is the dial.

What tilt cannot do now

With curvature gone, every card shares a normal, so no amount of tilting can light one card more than the card beside it. Variation across the hand comes from the pattern being drawn in page space — the rules run continuously across the whole fan — not from the lighting. That is the trade for "always illuminated", and it is why the twist moves the grain rather than the brightness.

Still open

Whether one palette serves both stocks or they want separate defaults; whether AMBIENT at 0.58 is the right floor or the edge wants more contrast; and whether the rules should keep drifting at all when the phone is still — DRIFT to 0 answers that one directly.
