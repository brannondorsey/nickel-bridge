Foil trumps

Four ways to make the trump suit catch the light — Balatro's own shader math, re-lit for a hand of thirteen cards on a paper table. Every card on this page is live; drag the lamp.

§ 1

The rig

Balatro seeds its shimmer per card — every foil card carries its own phase, so a row of them twinkles independently. That is right for a game where cards arrive one at a time. A bridge hand is thirteen cards held as one object, and thirteen independent twinkles read as static rather than as a surface.

So the field here is anchored in page space, not card space: the interference pattern is computed from where a pixel sits relative to one lamp, which means it runs continuously across the whole fan and keeps running across the gap onto the table. Cards near the lamp blaze, cards at the far end of the fan stay quiet, and the band travels the hand as one thing. Variant II keeps Balatro's per-card seeding so you can see the difference.

Motion

Fan curvature is the one invented quantity. A fan is drawn flat but held curved, so each card is given a surface normal that rotates with its place in the spread — at 0 the hand is a flat sheet and the whole thing lights at once, which is the tell that the effect is painted on rather than reflected.

§ 2

The table

Spades are trumps. Five of them in hand, one on the table where South ruffed the diamond lead — so you can read the same treatment on a card lying flat under the lamp and on cards held in a curve, at once.

One thing the fan's geometry decides for you. A card in hand is about sixty per cent hidden behind the card to its right, so only the exposed sliver can carry foil — and that turns out to be a gift rather than a limit. Trump placement already puts the trump block at the left of the fan, so the slivers are contiguous and the whole suit reads as one foil band down the edge of your hand. A card tabled to the trick, or lying in dummy, gets its full face.

§ 3

Four directions

Each strip below runs its own variant regardless of the lever above, so they can be read side by side. Three trumps and two plain cards in every strip — the contrast is the point.

I

The foil stamp

Hot foil on card stock, 1926. The conservative reading.

foil.fs's two interference terms at low gain, with the rainbow thrown away: the output is remapped onto a three-stop metal ramp — cool ink, verdigris, pale gold — and confined to a stamped plate inset from the card's edges. It never touches the corner index.

This is the only one of the four that is period-plausible. Hot foil stamping is a real 1920s printing process, so a foiled trump reads as a differently printed card rather than a magic one, which is the register the rest of the app is in — flat ink on paper, no gradients, one accent colour.

Cost: it is quiet enough that at a glance across a 390px phone you may not be sure which suit is trumps, which is the one job this feature has.

II

The hologram

Balatro, straight — polychrome.fs, per-card seed, full saturation.

The reference point, ported honestly: the HSL hue field from polychrome.fs, hue rotated by a moving noise field, saturation floored at 0.6, each card carrying its own phase offset exactly as the game does it.

Worth having on the board because it is what was asked for and because it makes the per-card-seeding argument visible: watch the strip and the five cards read as five separate objects that happen to be adjacent, not as one hand.

Cost: this is the variant that eats the ink. Rank and suit glyphs sit inside the lit area and lose contrast on every bright pass — the knockout mask is deliberately weak here so you can see the failure the other three are designed around.

III

The lamp

foil.fs intact, but lit by one source in the room. The recommendation.

Balatro's foil interference — the same four fac terms, the same blue push — with two changes. The pattern's origin is the lamp rather than each card's own centre, so it is one continuous field; and the whole thing is gated by a specular term computed from the card's fan normal, so a card only ignites when it happens to be facing the light.

The result is the thing that was actually asked for: move the lamp along the hand and the shine walks the fan, two or three cards at a time. Cards on the table, lying flat, catch it at a different moment from the ones in your hand.

Cost: the most expensive to reason about. The shine depends on lamp position, fan position and time, so "why is that card dull" has three answers — and a screenshot of it can look like nothing is happening.

IV

The grating

holo.fs's ruled diffraction lines, ignited only on the specular band.

holo.fs carries a term nothing else does: a set of ruled lines at three angles that behave like a diffraction grating. Drawn in page space they run straight across the whole hand, which is the strongest signal on this board that the thirteen cards are one surface.

The hue field is inherited from holo.fs as well, but it is hard-gated by the specular term — off everywhere except a narrow band, so the hand is mostly plain paper with one bright rule sweeping across it.

Cost: fine lines on a phone at devicePixelRatio 3 alias badly when the band moves slowly. Needs either a derivative-based width or a deliberate speed floor.

§ 4

The rule

Which cards foil, and when, is a separate decision from how they look — and it is the half that has to be exactly right, because a card that shines when it is not trumps is worse than no feature at all.

Which cards

Every card of the trump suit, wherever a card face is drawn: your fan, a tabled dummy fan, the trick. Trump comes from trumpForDisplay — the one place the app already converts a strain to a suit — so foil and the trump-left sort can never disagree about which suit it is.

When it starts

At the same beat the contract settles, which is the beat "The Draw" already owns. The trumps are singled out and travel to the left of the fan; landing lit is one event rather than two.

Never

On a no-trump contract (there is no trump suit to mark), during the auction (the contract is not settled), and on the E/W dummy rail, which prints ranks as text and has no card face to light.

Off by default

A new row on the settings gate — a two-segment lever beside Trump placement, the switch it is a companion to. Purely a client treatment: the server never reads it, and nothing about scoring, robots or replay changes either way.

§ 5

What it costs

The ink

This is the real risk and it is not solvable by turning the gain down. The card is DOM text under a blended canvas, so anything lit sits over the rank and suit. Every variant here knocks the corner index out of the mask — which is also the honest period answer, since a foil plate covers a field and not the type. Variant II shows what happens without it.

Reduced motion

A moving specular band is exactly what prefers-reduced-motion is about. FROZEN above is the fallback: the field is evaluated once and held, so a foiled trump still reads as a different surface without anything travelling. Not a degraded mode — a still photograph of one.

Night stock

The night card face is an ink-plate negative — dark stock, light glyphs — and it is deliberately low-contrast against the panel. Foil there has to add light rather than both add and subtract, or the card stops reading as a rectangle at all. Switch CARD STOCK to NIGHT and check every direction; III and IV hold up best.

The overlap

One overlay canvas has no idea which card is painted on top of which. Left alone, a trump's plate paints straight across the neighbour stacked above it — the ♠4's foil landing on the K♥ beside it, which is exactly what the first build of this board did. Each quad is clipped to the span between its own left edge and the next card's, which is geometry fanLayout.ts already computes.

One canvas

One fixed <canvas> for the page, cleared to mid grey and composited with hard-light, which is a no-op wherever nothing is drawn. That is why the fan, the trick and all four strips can light from one context and one field. A per-card layer would need thirteen contexts, or thirteen copies of a pattern that is supposed to be continuous.

The brand

Stated plainly: the design system says flat printed paper, no gradients, almost no shadow, max ink and minimum colour. Directions II and IV are arguing with that directly. I is the one that does not — and the reason I is on the board is that "make it look like foil" and "make it look like this app" have a real answer in 1926 printing, not only a compromise.

Open, for you

Which numeral — and does the lamp belong to the room or to the device? PLACED and POINTER are the same rig with a different input, but TILT changes what the feature is: the hand stops being lit and starts being held up to a light, which is a stronger idea and a permission prompt on iOS.

§ 6

Source material

The maths on this page is adapted from Balatro's own edition shaders — the LÖVE/GLSL files shipped in the game's resources/shaders/. Variant I and III descend from foil.fs, II from polychrome.fs, IV from holo.fs. The dissolve_mask half of each file is card-destruction machinery with no counterpart here and is dropped.
